# Cast, Arcs, and Relationships — The Long Siege

## Jeanne de Vauréal — Duchess of Vauréal, 22 (POV anchor)
Dark brown hair usually braided and veiled; grey eyes; thin scar at her left eyebrow from a childhood fall with Thiébaut. Reads Latin, keeps her own ledgers, trained by her father's clerics in law and accounts, taught to ride and hunt by his grooms. Never permitted to learn arms beyond the dagger. Controlled, observant, stubborn about precedents and legal wording; grieves in private because the state never adjourns.
- Wants: to die having surrendered nothing her father built.
- Need: to learn that trust is not abdication.
- Misbelief: to be fully known is to be made manageable; love is a lever someone will pull.
- Arc: defensive sovereignty → conditional alliance → shared command → chosen vulnerability without loss of self.
- Habits: checks doors and locks; keeps a small knife; writes letters she never sends; counts things under stress (steps, coins, bells).

## Thiébaut — Duke of Lorraine, 27 (second POV)
Fostered at Aubecourt from age 9 to 20 as hostage-pledge of a peace treaty. Lean, weathered, a soldier's hands; old wound in the left shoulder that stiffens in cold and limits his sword arm. Dry, low-voiced, listens more than he speaks; keeps accounts of his own and knows every mercenary captain's price. Has held Lorraine through six winters of raids with no money and no allies.
- Wants: a Lorraine that cannot be dismembered whenever Paris sneezes.
- Need: to stop carrying guilt alone and trust Jeanne with the truth and with command.
- Misbelief: protection means swallowing blame; he is not permitted to be the person someone chooses freely.
- Arc: guarded strategist → rival in council → partner under fire → man who lets himself be known and forgiven.
- Habits: tests blade edges with his thumb; hums old Aubecourt songs without noticing; never sits with his back to a door.

## The rupture — locked canon (revealed Ch.18)
When Jeanne was 15 and Thiébaut 20, during a three-day storm that shut the court indoors, they crossed from childhood love into spoken vows: one night in the deserted solar, clothed, kissing, planning. Discovered at dawn by the then-secretary Foulques and the guard. Duke Hugues's terms to Thiébaut: trial for the ravishment of the heiress (death), Jeanne to a convent of strict observance, and her maid Lisette flogged and branded for carrying messages — unless Thiébaut signed a written renunciation and performed it publicly. He did: before the assembled court he called her a foolish child, swore no promise existed, and renounced all Vauréal alliance. He was forbidden on pain of the others' punishments ever to explain. Jeanne knows only the public scene. Thiébaut knows the threats were real but not that Foulques kept the signed agreement. Foulques has carried the document and his guilt for seven years.

## Hugues de Vauréal — late duke (flashbacks only)
Brilliant unifier, cold father, brilliant battlefield gambler who finally lost one throw at Roches-Noires. Loved Jeanne as an heir, never as a child. His corpse is ransomed back in Chapter 1.

## Vauréal court
- **Maître Foulques**, 55, chancellor, cleric in minor orders. Keeper of the rupture document. Cautious, brilliant at precedent, quietly eating himself alive.
- **Dame Oriabel**, 40, widowed cousin, chief lady of the household. Warm, unsentimental, runs everything from linen to etiquette; Jeanne's nearest thing to family.
- **Lisette**, 22, maid since childhood; message-runner in the old love affair; quick, fearless, network of servant whisperers across every city.
- **Enguerrand de Brive, Marshal of Vauréal**, 63. Old-school cavalryman, loyal to the dynasty, skeptical of the Lorraine marriage and of gunpowder.
- **Captain Rostaing**, 28, cavalry captain. Brave, brutal, believes terror is strategy; his village massacre (Ch.14) forces Jeanne's hardest judicial test.
- **Gui de Hautperthuis**, 45, richest border lord. Charming, slow-smiling, connected by a grandmother to the ducal line; defects in Ch.17; captured Ch.23; tried Ch.24.
- **Othon d'Erquelines**, 50s, count on the imperial marches. Dithers, auctions homage, finally bought in Ch.22 at a foul price.

## The cities
- **Maître Coppens**, 60, dean of the Sainterre cloth guild. Rebel but a contract man; wants the charter more than he wants revolution; becomes Jeanne's unlikely civic partner.
- **Willem de Key**, 30s, weaver demagogue. Wants the whole order overturned; escapes to the French (Ch.13), returns as collaborator during the siege (Ch.19), dies in the relief.
- **Brother Anselm**, 40s, Franciscan-trained preacher in Grisay. Apocalyptic, sincere, dangerous; arrested Ch.13.
- **Van Maerlant**, Malemort port boss. Whoever controls the river controls him.
- **Zaccaria Dolfin**, 48, Venetian banker, head of the Sainterre fondaco. Civilized, contract-pious, never makes a loan without a lien; respects Jeanne's head for figures.

## Lorraine side
- **Jean, the Bastard of Lorraine**, 32, Thiébaut's illegitimate elder half-brother and right arm. Blunt, funny, loyal; wounded in the Harchies ambush.
- **Abbé de Viray**, 50s, Thiébaut's chancellor-priest. Shrewd, gentle, the only man who dares lecture the duke.
- **Claire de Sombernon**, 27, widowed lady, briefly Thiébaut's mistress in the years after the rupture. Arrives as a refugee in Ch.12. Intelligent and honorable; not a villain — her presence forces Jeanne's jealousy and Thiébaut's honesty.
- **Perrin**, 14, Thiébaut's page-squire.

## Enemies and envoys
- **Sire de Graçay**, 50s, French royal lieutenant. Cold professional, no sadism, no mercy either; respects Jeanne as an adversary.
- **Captain de Marcenay**, French free-company captain. Cruel, greedy, author of the sack in Ch.17 and the menace in Ch.20; killed in the relief of Sainterre.
- **Herr von Zollern**, imperial envoy. Polite, penniless promises.
- **Anne de France**, Madame la Régente. Offstage voice through letters: terrifyingly competent, always reasonable, always closing a door.

## Relationship engine
Jeanne–Thiébaut trajectory: strangers with old wounds (1–6) → spouses in form (7–8) → working partners (9–11) → friction and jealousy (12–14) → forged trust (15–16) → treason and the truth (17–18) → tested love under siege (19–21) → joint rule (22–25).
Jeanne–Foulques: reverence → disillusionment → a harder, adult forgiveness.
Jeanne–Coppens: enemies → treaty partners → mutual civic respect.
Jeanne–Hautperthuis: family feeling → betrayal → judgment of kin.
Thiébaut–Jean: easy brotherhood, comic counterweight.
