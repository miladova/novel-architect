# Style, Voice, and Rules — The Long Siege

## Narrative mode
Close third person, past tense. Jeanne is the anchor POV (21 of 25 chapters); Thiébaut gets Ch.4, Ch.15, and shared/split chapters 5, 21, 23. One viewpoint per scene; scene breaks marked with a blank line and a centered "* * *" rendered by the build. No head-hopping within scenes. POV character may not narrate another character's interior — only read faces and guess.

## Prose density and texture
- Literary but propulsive. Long, clause-rich sentences allowed; vary against short declaratives, especially in action and grief.
- Sensory grounding every scene: at least two physical senses, drawn from period reality (wool, tallow, iron, wet stone, horse sweat, incense, mud).
- Period detail carried by action and object, never by lecture. A reader should learn how a cannon is loaded by watching it happen.
- Emotion rendered through body, gesture, object, and withheld speech; name an emotion only when the character would name it.
- Dialogue roughly 35–45% of scene length. Characters are articulate, guarded, and often funny in a dry way; subtext always.

## Diction
- Period-flavored modern English: no "thee/thou" spelling, no fake medieval syntax, no modern idiom (no "lifestyle," "trigger," "cycle," "energy," "space" as metaphor, "toxic," "messaging," "platform").
- Permitted period vocabulary: homage, liege, suzerain, dower, charter, alderman, guild, ordinance, men-at-arms, gendarmes, pike, arquebus, bombard, fondaco, éc u, gros, league, palfrey, destrier, solar, garderobe, rushes, chaperon, kirtle, cote-hardie, houppelande.
- French/Flemish terms untranslated where context explains them; no italics beyond first use.
- Numbers in prose; figures and dates in ledgers/letters may stay numeric.

## Romance and sex (Mature / 18+)
- Explicit scenes at Ch.7, 11, 16; others dissolve or stay off-page. Sex always advances character knowledge or power dynamics, never decorative.
- Bodies described specifically, without romance-clinic euphemism and without pornography's inventory rhythm. Consent, awkwardness, and consequence included.
- Sexual menace (Ch.20) is about threat and defense, not titillation; the assault does not happen on-page.

## Violence
- Battle is confusion, logistics, mud, and aftermath more than choreography. Wounds have infection, smell, and cost. Dead bodies are somebody's tenants.
- Executions (Ch.24) rendered as law and grief, not spectacle.

## Structure habits
- Each chapter opens on a concrete image or action, not a summary.
- Chapter endings: changed state, not cliffhanger gimmick. A decision made, a letter opened, a person altered.
- Scenes should have a want, an obstruction, and a turn. No scene that only conveys information.
- Weave backstory in sensory fragments; the rupture is shown from Jeanne's misreading (Ch.3), Thiébaut's guilt (Ch.4), then truth (Ch.18).

## Forbidden patterns
- "couldn't help but", "a mixture of emotions", "the weight of the moment", "little did she know", "it was worth noting", "for a moment", "in that moment" (max one per chapter), "her heart skipped", "time stood still", "held her breath" (max one), "eyes flashing", "jaw tightening" (max one), "without thinking", "something shifted inside", "the air was thick with".
- No em-dash stacking: max one em-dash per paragraph; prefer commas, colons, or separate sentences.
- No opening sentences with "-ing" participial clauses more than once per chapter.
- No repeated gesture tics across a chapter (characters must not keep raising brows, clenching fists, or exhaling).
- No rhetorical questions in narration (dialogue excepted).
- No chapter titles used inside the prose.
- Avoid "very", "quite", "rather", "suddenly", "somehow", "almost" except where removing them changes meaning.

## Paragraph and dialogue mechanics
- Paragraphs mostly 1–6 lines; one-sentence paragraphs reserved for impact, max two per scene.
- Dialogue tags: said/asked as defaults; action beats preferred; no adverbial tags ("she said sharply").
- Unspoken thoughts in italics, sparingly.
- Letters and documents rendered as indented italic blocks with a provenance line.

## Continuity anchors
- Track season, light, and weather per scene against timeline.yaml.
- Money, distances, and travel times must respect world_bible rules.
- Keep named minor characters consistent; check terms.yaml before coining a new name.
- Wounds persist: Thiébaut's shoulder, Jean's pike wound, Jeanne's pregnancy from Ch.20.
