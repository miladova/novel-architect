# World Bible — The Long Siege

## Year and frame
Opens March 1484. Real frame: Kingdom of France under the boy king Charles VIII, governed by his elder sister Anne de France (Madame la Régente / Madame la Grande). Holy Roman Emperor Frederick III III, distant and tight-fisted; his son Maximilian of Habsburg operates in the Burgundian Netherlands. Printing exists but is rare outside the great cities; a single press operates in Sainterre. Faith is uniform late-medieval Catholicism; relics, indulgences, pilgrimages, and sermon politics are normal.

## The Duchy of Vauréal (fictional)
A composite dominion assembled in three generations by purchase, inheritance, marriage, and conquest, straddling the old Franco-Imperial marches. Wealth sources:
- Wool and finished cloth from the three northern cities (the white broadcloths of Sainterre are traded as far as Venice and the Hanse ports).
- Tolls on the river Ortelle, the carrying trade between northern seas and the Rhône valley.
- Iron mines and forges around Grisay; saltworks in the western flats.
- Lombard and Venetian banking houses resident in Sainterre; the ducal treasury runs partly on credit.

### Vauréal territories
- **Aubecourt**, capital. Ducal palace-citadel of the **Haut-Tertre** rises on a limestone bluff over the Ortelle. Cathedral of Saint-Cyr. Walled lower town with guildhalls, the mint, the Mercer's Row, the Venetian house (Fondaco).
- **Sainterre**, largest merchant city, two days north. Cloth guilds dominate; a commune restive for forty years. Guild dean Maître Coppens; militant weaver Willem de Key; one printing press.
- **Malemort**, river port downstream. Shipyards, grain staple, fish, customs house. Boss: Van Maerlant. Pragmatic, follows whoever holds the river.
- **Grisay**, forge city in the iron hills. Fullers, smiths, radical preaching; Brother Anselm's stronghold.
- **Mont d'Or**, the great frontier fortress on the southern marches facing France. Key to the kingdom road; governor post is a trust test.
- **Pont-d'Ortelle**, fortified bridge town and toll station.
- **Harchies Forest**, old royal woodland spanning the western roads; smugglers, outlaws, charcoal burners; site of the winter ambush.
- **Roches-Noires**, battlefield in the south where Duke Hugues died.
- Lordships: **Brive** (loyalist marshal's county), **Hautperthuis** (rich border lordship, Gui the traitor), **Erquelines** (imperial-leaning count Othon), **Sombernon** (Claire's dower lands).

## Neighbors
- **France**: claims the southern Vauréal fiefs as held of the French crown; declares them forfeit after Roches-Noires under a feudal technicality (alleged failure of homage + "treasonable correspondence with the Emperor"). Royal lieutenant on the ground: Sire de Graçay. French heavy cavalry (gendarmes), francs-archers, Gascon infantry, Breton and Scots mercenaries, a growing artillery train.
- **Holy Roman Empire**: offers protection in return for homage and an imperial marriage suit. Envoy Herr von Zollern. Emperor Frederick rarely pays troops; promises are cheap.
- **Duchy of Lorraine (real title, fictional duke)**: Thiébaut's capital at Nancy. A poor, proud marchland repeatedly raided; holds the roads between France and the Rhine. Lorraine field strength: picked men-at-arms, the duke's personal ordinance company, mounted archers, hired Swiss and German pike.
- **Prince-Bishopric of Heurtois** (fictional Liège analog): church principality, neutral in theory, venal in practice; hosts the final peace conference.
- Burgundian Netherlands: offstage; guild revolts there (Flanders, Bruges) are the template Sainterre studies.
Mercenary market: Swiss confederate pike blocks, German Landsknechts, Italian arquebus men, Albanian stradiots, Scots. All must be paid on the nail or they defect.

## Society, law, custom
- Female rule is legal but treated as provisional; every council opens with the unspoken question of whom she will marry.
- Cities want **charters**: confirmed self-rule, elected aldermen, guild courts, fixed tolls, the right to wall and militia. Dukes grant charters under pressure and claw them back when strong.
- Feudal incidents: homage, wardship, relief payments, purveyance, the dowry/dower system. A duchess's dower lands are her independent revenue.
- Justice: seigneurial courts, city aldermen's courts, Church courts for clergy, oaths, ordeals long gone; torture used in serious secular cases; executions public (hanging, beheading for nobility, burning for heresy/sodomy).
- Marriage: consent-based in canon law but dynastic in practice; public bedding ceremony still customary among the high nobility.
- Daily texture: tallow and beeswax light; rushes and herbs on floors; glass only in palace and church windows; Latin mass; French at court, Flemish dialects in the northern cities, German among imperial envoys, Italian among bankers; hours rung by church bells; water often unsafe, wine and small beer drunk; bathing at stewes; fleas, lice, the smell of wool, dung, smoke.

## War in 1484
- Full plate harness for men-at-arms; lances couched, dismounted fighting common in sieges.
- Pikes (Swiss fashion) are defeating cavalry; Jeanne's father died partly because his cavalry charged prepared pike-and-gun positions.
- Artillery: wrought-bronze and wrought-iron bombards on ox-drawn carriages; slow, ruinously expensive, decisive against medieval walls; hand-cannons/arquebuses rare and weather-sensitive.
- Sieges dominate: walls, mines, saps, fascines, famine, disease (flux, camp fever). Armies cannot stay assembled long without pay and supply.
- Heraldry, banners, paroles, ransom of nobles, no quarter for common infantry in many fights.

## Names and language register
Court French naming; northern cities carry Flemish-flavored names. Currency: gold **écu au soleil**, silver **gros**, deniers. Distance in leagues; weight in marks and livres. Documents in Latin; court speech French. Keep dialogue period-flavored but readable — no fake "thee/thou" orthography, no modern slang.

## Hard canon rules
- No characters know the future; 1484 people do not know what the Reformation or printing will become.
- Messages travel at horse speed (Aubecourt–Paris ~5 days; Aubecourt–Nancy ~3 days; Sainterre–Aubecourt 2 days).
- Winter halts campaigning except short, desperate marches.
- Money is always finite; every army must be paid or it dissolves.
- Jeanne's mother died in childbed when Jeanne was nine; her younger brother did not survive. No siblings.
