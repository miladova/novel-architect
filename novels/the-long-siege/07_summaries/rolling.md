# Rolling continuity summary

## Ch.1 The Ledger of the Dead (Mar 1484, Aubecourt)
Roches-Noires defeat: Hugues killed, army destroyed by Graçay's pikes/guns. Ransomed corpse returns; Jeanne (22) becomes duchess. Council: treasury 11k ecus, garrisons unpaid, French forfeit declaration over 3 southern fiefs, Sainterre guild articles, Malemort customs closed, Grisay preaching, Hautperthuis arriving, Erquelines courting Empire. Foulques raises the Lorraine marriage (treaty drafted pre-battle). Jeanne orders muster Apr 20, summons Dolfin and Coppens, seals herself as ruler. Funeral held; private grief in vault. Motif: blank new ledger, unsent letters casket, key on chain.

## Ch.2 The Vultures' Congregation (Apr 1, Aubecourt)
Four audiences: (1) French herald de Pleine-Selve delivers forfeit letter; Jeanne rebuts tenure law (1459 homage remission charter), answers in writing, buys a month. (2) Imperial envoy von Zollern offers hollow protection/garrisons/imperial prince marriage; declined with courtesy. (3) Venetian banker Dolfin: 40k ecu loan at 14%, liens on Malemort customs + Sainterre cloth duty 6 yrs; urges Lorraine marriage; first installment in a week. (4) Coppens presents Sainterre charter articles (aldermen, guild court, fixed tolls, militia, amnesty); Jeanne temporizes, warns against armed assemblies; de Key rising. Private: Hautperthuis angled for constableship; refused, instead charged to repair/provision Pont-d'Ortelle road at his cost; Lisette to watch his couriers. Ends: Thiébaut riding to Aubecourt himself, arrives within 10 days. Jeanne keeps treaty in casket with unsent letters.

## Ch.3 The Old Wound (Jeanne)
Muster field bleak: half quotas, no pike/guns, Brive favors contract army. Flashbacks: Thiébaut fostered age 9 (she 4); childhood alliance; her eyebrow scar from garden wall, Hugues caned him. The rupture night (age 15/20, storm, old solar, chaste vows and first kiss); Foulques caught them at dawn; public renunciation in great hall (he called it a child's infatuation, renounced all suits, rode away without looking back). Lisette (beaten for carrying notes) insists he looked like a man being broken. Council: imperial prince (no money/troops) vs Lorraine army (1100 men-at-arms, 2000 mounted archers, unbeaten ordinance). Jeanne accepts with 4 additions: doubled quota on invasion + joint command under her war council; dower paid to her treasurer; all Vauréal patents hers alone; wedding at Haut-Tertre, she styled duchess in her own right. Sends cold gate-terms letter signed "Jeanne." His column arrives a day early.

## Ch.4 Nancy's Bargain (Thiébaut POV)
Nancy poverty: false-bottom grain bins, duke pays garrison from his plate. Receives her letter (carried at his heart). Council votes marriage; Thiébaut imposed light-hand terms himself. Jean (Bastard half-brother) warned not to let old scandal eat alliance; Thiébaut refuses full confession ("forgiveness is not on offer"). Rides with 50 lances. Finds Vauréal border watch asleep at Nied bridge; burned hamlet (free companions, Hautperthuis lands spared); camps by Harchies; scouts confirm bandits ahead, paid to kill the Lorraine party; sees Aubecourt lights.

## Ch.5 The Road Between Armies
Dawn ambush in Harchies narrows: Lorraine lances trap the band; prisoner Beraud reveals factor from Pont-d'Ortelle with French gold, commission for Thiébaut's rings/seal; hints lord's steward (unwritten name = Hautperthuis). At Aubecourt: Jeanne in steel-grey gown; formal gate meeting; Thiébaut kneels; reports Harchies privately to Foulques/Brive, withholds name pending proof. Adjacent chambers, shared locked wall; both sit awake. Next morning: he critiques fixed gun carriages (offers Lorraine patterns), apple-debt callback (six not seven). Gallery exchange: he says both girl and duchy brought him; she says governments easier than history. Treaty council set.

## Ch.6 The Treaty of Everyone But Them
Contract haggling: Thiébaut concedes joint war council under her head, 10% dower penalty + saltworks lien, her patents over all captains, separate regencies/Hainaut model, her sovereign style. He privately says he came for the woman who keeps her own ledgers, hints the renunciation "was not what it looked like" but won't force the door; names Foulques as one of the people in that truth. Dolfin: 10k first installment, interest falls to 12.5%, urges quick Sainterre settlement. News: de Key seizes Sainterre cloth hall/gates; French take bridge-fort Saint-Vaast by treachery. Strategy: north first (Sainterre), Mont d'Or reinforced by reputation. Special-license wedding set Sunday.
## Ch.7 The Wedding at Haut-Tertre
Steel-grey gown, no white. Cathedral vows, joined arms. Feast politics: Hautperthuis gold cup, imperial marriage letter insult, Dolfin map gift. Bedding ceremony. Wedding night: Thiébaut insists nothing is owed, asks consent at every step; awkward, painful first time, then her pleasure; they share a bed; he hints his care came from knowing "what it costs to have no door that can be shut."
## Ch.8 Two Heads, One Pillow
Breakfast table = merged government. Clash: Thiébaut reorganizes host without patents; she forces private-arguments/public-one-voice rule, patents stay hers; Hautemer guns but Van Brock keeps powder seal. Charter framework: elected aldermen, city court, 6-yr fixed tolls, licensed militia (ducal officers, arsenal powder), war taxes; press licensing clause. Foulques admits he holds a secret document re: the old scandal in his own keeping, refuses to show it yet. Married nights: exchanged true accounts; she begins to trust him. Mont d'Or dispatch: French investment imminent; army marches.
## Ch.9 The Charter and the Cannon
Army at Sainterre (White Field). Coppens smuggles out: de Key holds 3 gates/cloth hall, 4000 weavers, French letters from Graçay. Jeanne enters city with 20 lances, speaks to square. Riot: de Key's armed weavers attack; one Lorraine soldier + 4 weavers killed (16-yr-old fuller boy); de Key flees to French. Guilds accept charter; Coppens first lead alderman. Jeanne licenses printer Verhoeven for weekly news sheet. She funds pension for dead boy's mother from privy purse.
## Ch.10 South to the Frontier
March discipline; burned-hamlet woman bought dog/crossbow. Mont d'Or: Captain Valvert, 61+ men, well-armed, unassailable. Thiébaut's war = make French occupation expensive/clock. Jeanne's gunnery lesson, fires Marguerite accurately. Campfire: Sarrebourg story (Thiébaut's shoulder, farrier reset), his return to Lorraine at 20 with nothing. Dawn French probe at south ford: battery breaks it, prisoners, paybooks; she watches from windmill. Field court: thief boy sent to pioneers not hanged.

## Ch.11 The Weight of Bodies
Assault on Saint-Vaast. Proclamations protect civilians; priest/alderman parley. Two-day bombardment, dawn breach; Jeanne runs the hospital tents (saw, vinegar, coals), page Philippe Aubert (14) dies in her arms. Town taken, commandant de Noailles captured; 3 looters hanged, sack prevented. Names-of-the-dead ledger started; stretcher-bearers decorated (silver lamp badge). Night: grief-driven love scene; they vow to keep the book together.
## Ch.12 The Banker's Ledger (winter 1484-85, Aubecourt)
Campaign cost 78k. Estates assembly: Jeanne imposes subsidies (lords 1/15, towns 1/12, clergy 1/20), no committee. Lisette: Hautperthuis steward met French factor at Heurtois; ex-French sergeants planted as tenants. Dolfin: another 30k loan at 12%, iron tithe of Grisay as 6-yr security, audit right; gives sealed list of lords with foreign bank balances. Claire de Sombernon arrives as refugee; Jeanne jealous; direct woman-to-woman talk, Claire becomes useful court intelligence. Thiébaut fully discloses the past affair (brief, kind, ended pre-treaty); pact: no locked rooms, tell each other first. Grisay dispatch: Anselm's preaching turning violent.
## Ch.13 The Preacher's Fire (Grisay)
Clerk Rémy beaten to death by Anselm-incited crowd in church porch. Jeanne debates him in the iron market (Deborah/Huldah texts; tax pays for walls). Attempt on her life in street; Lisette fells attacker with shop sign. Washerwoman witness: Anselm said "let the account books be answered in blood," watched without calling watch. Arrested under city incitement statute (not heresy), tried in city court, condemned, Jeanne commutes to silent hard labor at Malemort. De Key exposed (French gold in his rooms), flees to French. Smiths get charter terms; forges reopen.
## Ch.14 Letters from Paris (Aubecourt, Feb 1485)
Captured French couriers: spring two-prong plan, "the party known to us" (=Hautperthuis), French army 3 months unpaid. Regent Anne's silky peace letter (southern fiefs as dowry, French garrison at Saint-Vaast, Heurtois conference); Jeanne accepts talks to stall. Rostaing burned village Argnon (27 dead incl. 14 children) for sheltering scouts; public trial, death sentence commuted to demotion to common man-at-arms at Mont d'Or under Valvert. Bride convinced. Foulques flinches: speaks of a man who used law against "a girl who had done nothing" seven years ago. Jeanne orders Lisette to find the green case. Council plans February Harchies relief of Sarrebourg; Jeanne adds sledge-gun convoy via western track.

## Ch.15 The Frost March (Thiébaut POV, Feb 1485)
Light column through Harchies; defeats French francs-archers camp, then receives 200-lance charge at road bend (pike hedge). Jean takes a pike meant for Thiébaut's throat, near-fatal shoulder/chest wound, fever. Jeanne herself rides with sledge-gun convoy via western track; guns arrive at Sarrebourg relief on the promised eighth day; gascons withdraw. Storm night in charcoal hut: he nearly reveals rupture secret but refuses to smuggle it past her judgment; promises whole truth in spring; she reveals Foulques's green case and they vow to open it together. Jean recovers.
## Ch.16 The Hunting Lodge (spring 1485)
Spring advantage: French unpaid, Heurtois talks stalling. Day at Vertbois lodge, no clerks: hawking, childhood memories, first real laughter; deep talk of fathers and rule (her fear of becoming Hugues; his answer: she keeps the book of the dead). Night of tender love. Back at court: Dolfin renews credit at 11% (Venice believes she wins). Coppens letter: charter thriving, taxes early. Erquelines openly brings imperial marriage/garrison offer seeking eastern lieutenancy; refused. Hammer: Hautperthuis seizes Mont d'Or by treachery during "inspection"; Valvert killed; French vanguard inside. Full muster ordered; attainder papers begun.

## Ch.17 Treason's Market (summer 1485)
Lisette's map of the fifth column (planted tenants, factor, 7 lords). Jeanne refuses fire reprisals: legal lease-breaking, tenants relocated to Grisay. Attainder prepared as paperwork case (corruption of the marches). Hautperthuis writes silky denial; she sends formal 30-day summons. Marcenay's free company sacks weaving town Sainte-Florence: 31 militiamen hanged, 42 women held, 3 dead incl. 12-yr-old girl, 400 looms smashed (Dominican friar's book of names). Brive/Jean column retakes it; Marcenay flees; named sergeants hanged; discipline bounty. Jeanne funds rebuilding + hospital. Foulques cracks, asks to unburden. Erquelines panics; young baron Villers confesses and is forgiven.
## Ch.18 What the Chancellor Carried (Aubecourt)
Foulques has a heart seizure. Green case confession: Hugues's terms — trial/ravishment charges, convent order (never canceled, blank date), Lisette flogging/branding. Thiébaut signed renunciation at dawn (wept), performed great-hall cruelty to protect Jeanne and Lisette, forbidden all contact forever. Documents: renunciation, marginal threats, convent order, Foulques's notes ("the question is not the truth but the story"), old portrait. Jeanne confronts Thiébaut in the old solar; full account; no instant forgiveness; separate chamber for now; warns never again to choose what she can bear. Foulques resignation refused; penance: write full sealed account, run school for war orphans (Latin/accounts/law) named for her mother.

## Ch.19 The Siege of Sainterre (June 1485)
Graçay invests Sainterre; Jeanne stays by choice for charter council. Thiébaut keeps field army mobile, raids convoys; arrow-post letters begin. Bombardment; rationing/flux; Verhoeven prints siege news sheet + names of dead. de Key returns as French-paid collaborator offering surrender; guilds reject. First assault repelled (97 city dead). Merchant traitors expelled; 5 de Key conspirators hanged by city court for water-gate plot. French herald gets "The charter!" reply.
## Ch.20 The Sallies
Day 40: horse meat, hunger. Jeanne pregnant (~2+ months; Sister Margriet confirms; special rations shared with all women/children). Marcenay leads mine-breach assault on river wall, takes outer ward, his men trap women in old church crypt. Jeanne leads 400 women in formed column (crossbows/pikes), retakes church, marks a named Sainte-Florence sergeant. Garrison throws French back; 41 dead. Powder for only 10 days; requisition saltpeter, fixed firing rates, letters by arrow/swimmer; countermine floods French gallery. Tells Thiébaut of pregnancy via arrow letter.

## Ch.21 The Breaking of the Siege
Relief plan: Brive feints south with banners; main column through river woods with Harchies sledge guns; Dolfin-paid Landsknechts (von Eppeln) hold French gendarmes at pike square. City opens water gate at rocket signal; militia attacks French works from inside. Rostaing (common man-at-arms since Ch.14) kills Marcenay in battery ditch, badly wounded; his debt marked paid, captaincy restored. de Key hanged by his own weavers' company. Graçay retreats in good order. Reunion in cloth hall; Rostaing/Bride dressing-station scene; night reunion; Jeanne vows no locked-room child-rearing.
## Ch.22 The Price of Crowns (Jul-Oct 1485)
Fixed famine prices; rebuilding. Dolfin: debt 94k; new 40k loan for Mont d'Or; Sainterre customs become 6-yr annuity (casting vote hers), war taxes written into covenants but voted by cities. Enlarged charter: permanent aldermen, wider city court, 10-yr tolls, permanent war tax; nobility rage, she answers with the wall/pikes speech. Guild of Saint Margaret: women's armed guild chartered. Rostaing reinstated, gave back-pay to Argnon well. Erquelines bought with 21-yr toll-bridge monopoly (her officers, lapse on disloyalty). Foulques's orphan school for 40 girls. Pregnancy ~5-6 months; winter Mont d'Or siege planned.

## Ch.23 The Fortress at Mont d'Or (Dec 1485-Jan 1486)
Winter siege of Mont d'Or (French commandant Chastel, 600 men; Hautperthuis inside). Countryside supplies the army at fixed prices. Gunner duel; Hautemer killed by keep gun; Jeanne (8 months pregnant) takes over the battery under fire using Van Brock's tables, silences embrasure. Groom Colas tries to poison Thiébaut; page Perrin smells it, foils plot; chain leads to Hautperthuis's steward. Assault: two mines breach SW angle; Jean concussed; Thiébaut's left shoulder re-broken; Rostaing leads van; fortress taken. Hautperthuis captured in donjon, held for law. Chastel treated honorably; Valvert's body sent home with memorial gate stone.

## Ch.24 The Price of Crime (Jan-Feb 1486)
Heurtois peace conference (Graçay leads France; Von Zollern/Venice observe; Dolfin present). Treaty of Heurtois: Thiébaut's peacetime personal homage for three southern fiefs only, no garrisons, no liege bond; forfeit annulled; northern towns returned; city charters recognized in perpetuity as ancient customs; 25,000 écus "dowry of reconciliation" in three installments; prisoners returned without ransom; ordnance returned. Regent Anne de France behind the push. Then open printed peer trial of Hautperthuis at Aubecourt: Lisette's fifth-column map displayed; attainted as lordship, not bloodline; death sentence, children spared and raised at court under the orphan school. Jeanne's night cell visit: his motive nostalgia for the old order of unaccountable lords; his posthumous letter yields three new French court contacts. Dawn execution by sword (Nancy headsman); Jeanne attends veiled; private weeping after. Spring ratifications and demobilization; Rostaing given Mont d'Or command; peace proclamation printed in red-ink charter edition, homage in Paris deferred to autumn progress.
## Ch.25 The Ledger of Peace (spring-Sep 1486)
Peacetime ledgers: first war-debt installment redeemed at par through Dolfin; customs annuity servicing on schedule; demobilization paid in full. Spring circuit: Sainte-Florence hospital and rebuilt looms; Sainterre rebuilt, memorial wall of 411 names with charter set in bronze, council re-elected, charter re-sealed with treaty clause. Foulques asks to teach law two days a week at the orphan school; new chancellor's wing approved. Aveline born 28 April 1486, named for Jeanne's mother; female-line succession secured, no wardships; public nursing policy; July christening (Brive godfather, Claire godmother, Coppens's gift of first Sainterre cloth). Green-case original instruments burned; Foulques's sealed full account retained in private archive until Jeanne's death. Cathedral vault scene at father's tomb: she chooses the charters over his model of rule. Found-family court dinner. Dolfin returns to Venice leaving a factor; Perrin holds first high-table place. September epilogue: dual ledgers in the old chamber, the connecting door stands open, joined-arms margin mark as the new house seal.
